We have spent a lot of time sharing lessons from our years of marriage, and we started to feel we hadn't hung out with the singles as much! So this is for you.

Being on this side of marriage, one thing has become very clear to us: a lot of the issues in marriage start when people are single. We tell people all the time, whatever you don't deal with as a single, you will have to deal with as a married person if you want to be successfully married. The good news is that it's a lot easier to fix those things while you are single.

And just like there are issues in marriage that start when you are single, there are blessings you enjoy in marriage that start when you are single too. So picture a single man or a single woman. They're ready, they're of marriageable age, but there's nobody yet. Where should they start? Here is our roadmap.

## Start With the Right Perspective

What you believe about marriage shapes every decision you make. If you don't believe right, you open yourself to the wrong decision and become more susceptible to a counterfeit from the enemy. Three beliefs make a strong foundation.

### God has somebody for you

When you know God has someone for you, it changes how you think. You are not desperate in a negative way, and you will not sell yourself for something that is not of God. It is when people don't believe God has someone for them that they end up breaking His laws to get something.

Some people struggle to believe this because of their family of origin, their experiences, or their self-image. They don't feel pretty enough, handsome enough, wealthy enough, or intelligent enough. But God has somebody for you. That truth lets you relax, not into complacency, but into walking with Him.

### It is not locked into one person

Some people believe God has someone for them, and then the idea becomes paralyzing. *What if I miss that one person?*

Some people wrongly believe, "If this person doesn't respond, I'm stranded." But God will never strand your destiny! Jesus chose twelve disciples, and one of them was Judas. When Judas fell, Acts 1 records that another took his place. If God made provision for the apostles, one of the most central things He was doing on earth, how much more will He provide for you? Person A may be the person for this season. But if person A rejects the will of God, God can bring person B. God has someone for you, but your future is not held hostage by one individual's choices.

### God is invested in your marriage

God wants you to enjoy marriage. He is not holding out on you.

**Kemi:** In my single days, I was there. It was like, "Gosh, this is far from me." Maybe because of my family of origin, or what I had seen around me. It felt like, yes, God has settled me with my job and these other things, but this area of marriage is a difficult thing. That is not true. God wants each and every one of His children to enjoy the blessing of marriage.

He also has a vested interest in who you marry. Your spouse will likely be the greatest help or the greatest hindrance to you fulfilling God's purpose for your life. It is much harder to do God's will married to someone who does not want to do it, and much easier with someone who keeps encouraging you to obey God. You don't have to talk God into giving you the right spouse. He wants the work of the kingdom done.

Once you understand that, you pray differently, and you stop resisting God.

**Tayo:** Sometimes I run into people who say, "I feel God is leading me toward this person, but I don't like them." I understand. I want people to be real. But if the One who loves you and made you says, "This is it," and you're not feeling it, your prayer shouldn't be, "God, give me somebody else." Your prayer should be, "God, deliver me from this insanity!" If I don't like what God is giving me, something is wrong with me. We have all been there, where God wants us to do something and it's not our taste or our preference, and we need His help. One scripture the Lord gave me once when I was dealing with this was Philippians 2:13: it is God who works in you both to will and to do of His good pleasure. God can work on your desire and your willingness.

Look at Joseph and Mary. God was intentional about the two people He put together.

> Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not wanting to make her a public example, was minded to put her away secretly.
> Matthew 1:19 (NKJV)

Everybody talks about Mary, and rightly so. But Joseph was a critical part of that story. He believed in his heart that Mary had wronged him, and was lying on top of it. By the law, she could have been stoned. Yet he didn't want to embarrass her or take vengeance. And when God spoke, Joseph obeyed quickly. "Go here, Joseph." Yep. "Now go there." He aligned himself with Mary when it was not popular and would probably have been ridiculed. He helped Mary fulfill her destiny, and Mary was aligned with him in fulfilling their destiny as a family. Thank God for people like that! Why wouldn't God choose him for such an assignment?

## Focus on Being the Right Person

There are two sides to a good marriage: finding the right person and being the right person. Being the right person is probably the more important one, because when you are the right person, it is easier to find, or be found by, the right person. Joseph's character is exactly why God could trust him with such an assignment.

Being the right person includes three things:

1. **Be who God made you to be.** Grow in character and in purpose. For some people, the distance between who God made them to be and who they are today is very far. Close that gap.
2. **Be where God wants you to be.** You cannot do the will of God everywhere. When Paul wanted to go into Asia, the Spirit stopped him, and then Paul saw a vision of a man from Macedonia calling for help. There is a place for you. Sentiment, wrong alliances, or simply your own desires can keep you in the wrong place.
3. **Do what God wants you to do in that place.** Some people are in the right place but not doing the right thing. Develop the fruit of the Spirit and use your gifts. If you can sing, sing. If you can write, write. If you can help, help.

**Tayo:** Sometimes the way people find a spouse is the funniest thing. I remember one man sharing his testimony with me. People were eating at church, and she was serving the food. When he came up, she gave him a little extra! And I'm sure, as a single man, he didn't always have good food. That's what caught his attention. But what would have happened if she wasn't there serving?

## Start Preparing Early

Bible history suggests Mary was probably around fifteen or sixteen when she was betrothed. We are not telling teenagers to get married. But preparation can start early, and parents can begin teaching their children what marriage is and what it is for.

Genesis 2 is a good place to start. What was God's intention for marriage? As a single adult, it is not too early to ask the Lord to open your eyes to see what He sees about marriage, and about *your* marriage. Whatever He puts on your heart, write it down and pray about it. When a passage of Scripture or a godly couple around you touches your heart, write down what stirred you and ask God for that grace.

And you don't have to come from a perfect family to believe for these things.

**Kemi:** In my single days, even though my background was a bit shaky, what the Lord did to help me was plant me among godly married couples. There were things about this couple and that couple that I just desired, and I would pray into it: "Lord, I'm trusting You that in our marriage we will enjoy this grace." There was one couple who used to teach and help other couples have a blessed marriage, and I just desired that. I had no clue I would be doing this today! I said, "Lord, I'm not going to limit myself to the past experiences of my family background. I look to You."

So it's not too early to understand what God originally intended. And when you see things that have become common in our culture but are contrary to that, go back to the Word: "Lord, is this consistent with Your Word or contrary to it?" Then align yourself accordingly.

## Practical Steps While You Wait

Even if you are not in a relationship and have no prospects, there is a lot you can do:

- **Get healed from the past.** If you have been hurt or damaged, recover before you enter a new relationship.
- **Become whole in God.** People who are not whole sometimes form ungodly attachments. Let God be sufficient, so the person who comes adds to your life instead of taking His place.
- **Understand your purpose.** When you know what God has called you to do, some people clearly will not fit, and you save yourself a lot of grief.
- **Learn to walk in love.** It is the most important skill for a happy marriage.
- **Build healthy friendships.** You need trusted relationships to help you vet a prospect. Ruth did not realize Boaz's field was a marital opportunity until Naomi pointed it out. Zipporah did not consider Moses until her father asked where the man who had helped them was.
- **Keep learning.** Get educated, grow in wisdom, and increase your capacity. Everybody appreciates good things.

## Recognize Your Season

There are seasons when, without doing anything differently, it feels like a spotlight is suddenly on you. That can be a strong indication that it is your season. When it comes, dig deeper into the things of God, because you need to hear His voice clearly. Think of it this way: the bee you are waiting for will come, but the flies will come too! They are all attracted to the same thing. You need the Holy Spirit to recognize which one is real and which ones are just noise. So pray, "Holy Spirit, I need clarity," and yield yourself to the Lord.

As pastors, we have seen ladies in a season where four or five people are pursuing them, and they get complacent. No, you need to pray! Don't entertain the wrong person, because then the right person might walk away. But don't be complacent either. Don't play unnecessarily hard to get, and don't think, "Well, I don't have time yet." God is the one who determines your season. Sometimes it comes when you thought you would be doing something else. When God brings something, make the most of it.

::: takeaways Your Roadmap at a Glance
- **Believe right.** God has someone for you, it is not locked into one person, and He is invested in your marriage.
- **Be the right person.** Be who God made you to be, where He wants you, doing what He called you to do.
- **Prepare now.** Learn God's purpose for marriage, write down what He shows you, and heal, grow, and build godly friendships.
- **Discern your season.** Stay close to the Holy Spirit so you recognize the real thing when it comes.
:::
