We have been walking through what we like to call God's recipes for a blessed marriage, digging into Scripture for the gems that help a marriage thrive. So far we have talked about submitting to one another in the fear of God (Ephesians 5:21), and about the instructions God gave wives and the instructions He gave husbands. God is very intentional and very specific. He knows that when a wife honors what He told her, and a husband honors what He told him, the marriage is blessed.

He also made room for the marriage where one person is putting in their best and the other is putting in a little. If that is you, keep pushing. Keep doing what God asked *you* to do. Don't become the police officer or the monitor for your spouse. Focus on your own part, trust God, and stay connected to a community that holds you both accountable.

Now we want to share something we call **the law of mutual edification.**

## Why Staying in Your Lane Is Not Enough

**Kemi:** The husband has his instructions and the wife has hers. But did you know that me focusing on my lane, and Tayo focusing on his, is not necessarily enough?

As believers, we all walk inside a big circle called the will of God. Inside that circle, I am supposed to submit to my husband and he is supposed to love his wife. We can both be doing exactly that, both of us fully in the will of God, and still not overlap. Two people standing in the same field, each doing their own thing.

The law of mutual edification says: stay within God's boundaries, and overlap as much as you possibly can. So it is not "I'm just going to submit to my husband" and "I'm just going to love my wife." It is doing those things *together*, in a way that blesses the other person.

**Tayo:** I always say this should be the default thinking in every marriage. If both of you don't think this way, somebody will get hurt and somebody will suffer. You can still have a marriage, yes. But if you want the best of marriage, you both have to think this way. And when you do, marriage becomes a multiplier.

Put plainly, the law of mutual edification means **the husband and the wife are both built up in the marriage, within the boundaries of God's Word.** Not one person enjoying the benefits. Both of you.

## What 1 Corinthians 7 Teaches

> Let the husband render to his wife the affection due her, and likewise also the wife to her husband. The wife does not have authority over her own body, but the husband does. And likewise the husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife does.
> 1 Corinthians 7:3-4 (NKJV)

Paul applies this to physical intimacy, but the principle reaches into everything in marriage.

**You signed up for an obligation.** When you decided to get married, you took on the responsibility of giving your spouse the affection due to them. See it that way: "I came into this marriage with that obligation."

**Now there are three of you.** We like to say the wife does not have authority over her own body *alone*. When you were single, you and the Lord had authority over your life. Once you marry, your spouse shares in that. And this is just as true for the husband as it is for the wife.

**Your thinking becomes joint.** What we have, we use for both of us. Paul was speaking about the body, but it doesn't stop there.

### Marriage adds an obligation

Later in the same chapter, Paul says the unmarried person cares about how to please the Lord, while the married person also cares about how to please their spouse (1 Corinthians 7:32-35). If you are not careful, you will hear what Paul is not saying, as if marriage were a bad thing. Keep reading and he tells you he is not putting a leash on anyone. He is simply saying that marriage adds an obligation.

When you are single, you have one person to please: the Lord. Once you choose to get married, that doesn't go away. Jesus is still Lord and He still comes first. But now you have taken on an extra obligation to please your spouse too. That is exactly why it is so dangerous to marry someone who doesn't want to please the Lord. Your attention gets divided.

So you take your spouse's preferences into consideration and you learn to bend toward them. That isn't always easy, because it may not be what you want. But as long as what your spouse prefers is not against the will of God and doesn't hurt the family, bend. Sometimes there will be some negotiating. And when you land on a decision that **pleases the Lord, pleases your spouse, and pleases you**, all three at once, you are standing right in the center of the law of mutual edification.

## Who Is Your Nearest Neighbor?

**Tayo:** I remember the first time I saw this scripture. I said, "Wow." When Jesus was asked about the greatest commandment, He answered:

> Jesus said to him, 'You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind.' This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like it: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.'
> Matthew 22:37-39 (NKJV)

First you love God, please God, and stay within His boundaries. But the second commandment matters too. Some unbalanced teaching says, "All I have to do is please God." If you are single, yes. If you are married, no.

The word *neighbor* means someone nearby. Who is your nearest neighbor? Your spouse. Loving your neighbor as yourself means you don't make a decision that favors you at your spouse's expense. You look for the decision that blesses and favors you both. Can you imagine the husband thinking that way and the wife thinking that way at the same time? It is such a sweet place to be in a marriage.

## "We Both Love the Lord, but We Don't See Eye to Eye"

Maybe you are sitting there thinking, "We weren't unequally yoked. We are both believers. We both love the Lord. But we just don't see eye to eye." Pray and ask God to knit your hearts together. Right now you may be two single individuals standing in the middle of God's will. Start working toward overlap, so that someone looking in from the outside sees one unit, not two.

That togetherness comes when the husband keeps asking, "How can I be a blessing to my wife?" and the wife keeps asking, "How can I be a blessing to my husband?" All of it on the foundation of a wife submitting and a husband loving.

**Kemi:** The more we have lived this way, the more one we have become. People tell us this, and even our children. One of them will go to Daddy with a question, he says, "Go ask Mom," and they say, "We already know what she's going to say. Exactly what you just said!" If you know me, you know I am very different from my husband. But so much of our thinking is now in alignment.

That is when Genesis 2:24 comes alive: a man shall be *joined* to his wife, and the two become one flesh. Imagine two people physically joined together. They would have to make decisions that work for both of them, because neither one can move without the other.

### There is a boundary

This doesn't mean agreeing with your spouse no matter what. In Acts 5, Peter asked Sapphira why she had agreed with her husband to lie to the Holy Spirit. God expected her *not* to go along with Ananias when he stepped outside His will. She should have said, "How dare you lie to the Holy Spirit?" Sometimes when we teach this, people take it to an extreme. Mutual edification always happens inside the boundaries of God.

## Make It the Default Everywhere

Most couples already make joint decisions about their children. The goal is to carry that same thinking into every other area. My body is not just my body. My time is not just my time. Paul used physical intimacy as his example, but he was really saying: seek to bless and please each other in every area. The more areas where you are joined under the will of God, the more you will enjoy your marriage.

If your marriage feels more like two roommates right now, you can start today:

- **Pray together.** Even after years of building this culture in our home, there are still times we both have to take something to God together before we see it the same way.
- **Talk as long as you need to.** It is not always, "Do you see what I'm saying? Okay, we're good." Sometimes it is a conversation over days, weeks, even months. Be committed to getting aligned, and don't get frustrated that you see things from different angles.
- **Never bully your spouse into agreement.** You may get temporary results, but you won't get a happy marriage. The goal is to be of one mind toward one another. It's not just me. It's us.

::: takeaways Try This Together
- **Ask the three-way question** before your next decision: does this please the Lord, bless my spouse, and bless me?
- **Name one area** where you have been living like roommates, and agree to start overlapping there.
- **Bend where you can.** If your spouse's preference is within God's will and doesn't hurt the family, lean toward it this week.
