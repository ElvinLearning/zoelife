Effective Communication in Marriage and Family: A Biblical Tool for Resolving Conflict

By Tayo and Kemi · October 13, 2026 · 15 min read

So far in our Conflict Resolution series, we have shared three tools. First, be sober-minded and have a biblical view of conflict and offense, with the right expectations of people. Second, embrace God's method for handling conflict. Third, mature in the love of God. When you mature in love, you get stronger muscles, for lack of a better term. You are more resilient, less easily offended, and less likely to offend others, because you care about them.

The fourth tool is one you can start practicing today: learn to communicate effectively.

We are not saying you can avoid every conflict. But effective communication keeps you out of unnecessary ones, helps you get out of the ones you are in, and keeps the rest from being blown out of proportion. Look at conflict in general: many conflicts are started by bad communication. Many are fueled by bad communication. Many are magnified by bad communication. We made a bad situation worse because we didn't know what to say, how to say it, or when. But on the flip side, good communication can resolve so many conflicts and tone down so many more.

A soft answer turns away wrath, But a harsh word stirs up anger.

Proverbs 15:1 (NKJV)

In other words, you can actually bring a conflict down just by learning how to speak.

What Gets in the Way

Before we get to the principles, it helps to know what distorts communication.

  • Noise. Person A says "one," and person B hears "one, two." Or you say A and they hear B. Or you say A and B, and they only hear B. Things get added and taken away. There is a lot of noise in our environment.
  • Over-communication. Sometimes people give others information they ought not to have. God doesn't give everybody the same level of information. When the disciples asked Jesus why He spoke to the crowds in parables, He told them the mysteries of the kingdom had been given to them. There are things God won't tell you until you are at that level.
  • Half-truths. Of all the problems in communication, this may be the biggest. A half-truth sounds like the truth, but it isn't. It's so common in our day, and the devil capitalizes on it. What caused man to fall was a half-truth: "Has God indeed said...?" There are so many phrases out there that sound very good, but if you take them and run with them, you will end up destroyed.
  • Sincere falsehoods. A lie is false information someone says knowing it is false. But many people say things in complete sincerity that simply aren't true, often because they were discipled wrongly. They believe it, they repeat it, and if you just go with it, it can be very destructive and lead to a lot of conflict.
  • The listener's perspective. If you are talking to a child, communicate at the level they understand. A man and a woman may see the same thing very differently. We have to be willing to explain until everybody is on the same page.

Once we are aware of all these things, we can focus on the principles that make communication clearer and more effective.

1. Learn to Listen

So then, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath; for the wrath of man does not produce the righteousness of God.

James 1:19-20 (NKJV)

If you really want to be good at communication, the first skill is learning to listen. Many people want to speak more than they listen. Again and again in Revelation, Jesus said, "He who has an ear, let him hear." God wants us to be good listeners. It's a skill everybody can grow in, but you have to train yourself.

And train yourself to hear not just what someone said, but why they said it. The spirit behind it. If you study the life of Jesus, you will find He often didn't answer what people asked. He answered the spirit behind what they asked.

Take John 3. Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews, came to Jesus by night and said, "Rabbi, we know that You are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs that You do unless God is with him." And Jesus answered, "Unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God." One day as we read that, we said, "But that wasn't the question!" Nicodemus never asked how to enter the kingdom. But Jesus saw his heart. He answered the why behind the statement, because He was listening.

If we learned this, we would avoid so much conflict. Many wives complain that their husbands aren't listening, and the husbands are sincerely confused. Many of them have no idea; they just didn't get it. The answer is learning to listen for the why, with the ears of your heart and not just your natural ears.

2. Confirm What You Heard

When you are listening, confirm what you understood. Don't just say, "Well, I'm listening." Say, "This is what I believe you are saying. Is that what you meant?" Repeat it back before you respond. Sometimes people are already answering before they have confirmed what the other person actually said. This is a very powerful tool.

The Bible tells us not to add to what God says and not to take away from it. Learn to be precise with what you hear. We are creatures of habit, so how we listen to people tends to become how we listen to God. People who don't hear man often don't hear God either, and they run off with their own interpretation of what He said.

After the resurrection, Jesus told Peter how he would die, and Peter turned around and asked, "What about John?" Jesus said, "If I will that he remain till I come, what is that to you?" And a rumor spread in the church that John would not die until Jesus came back. But that is not what Jesus said! John had to clarify it. Then John lived a long time on top of it. Imagine how shocked some people would have been after he died: "What Jesus prophesied didn't happen!" But He never said that. People ran off with their own interpretation because they didn't confirm what they heard.

3. Be Slow to Speak

Being slow to speak has been a real journey for some of us. The way some of us are wired, we are straight shooters! But we are learning. Sometimes just pausing before speaking makes such a big difference. "Oops, I'm so glad I didn't say that. It would have come out all wrong." So don't be too quick to speak, and don't be too sharp with your words either.

Someone once said God gave us two ears and one mouth so we would hear twice as much as we speak. We think that's a very good principle. Speak what you know. Some people speak to impress or to boast. Others jump from A to Z. A is a fact, but Z is their conclusion, and they state Z as fact with so much confidence that you're left asking, "Where did you get that from?"

It's okay to say, "I know A, and I think B." Paul did that. He said, in effect, "This the Lord commanded, and this is my own judgment." He could have said, "I'm an apostle, so whatever I say is the Lord." Instead he carefully separated what God said from his own opinion. We all have opinions and preferences, and it's okay to express them. Just don't make them the command of the Lord.

Be especially slow to speak evil of people. The book of Jude tells us that even the archangel Michael, when he contended with the devil, did not bring a reviling accusation. All he said was, "The Lord rebuke you!" Can you imagine? If there's anyone you could speak evil of, it's the devil. Yet Michael didn't. Sometimes we hear how people talk about other Christians, or a scandal breaks and people who don't know enough to comment jump in anyway. These things cause a lot of conflict.

4. Speak Life

A wholesome tongue is a tree of life, But perverseness in it breaks the spirit.

Proverbs 15:4 (NKJV)

It's not only about being slow to speak. It's about speaking life, words that edify and bless. The life in your words can actually quench conflict and quench offense. When you speak, speak life. Don't throw your words around carelessly. Jesus said we will give account for every idle word on the day of judgment. Not just evil words. Every idle word. Sometimes we haven't taken that seriously.

When Paul was teaching the church in 1 Corinthians 14 about speaking in tongues, he said that in church he would rather speak five words with his understanding, to teach others, than ten thousand words in a tongue. And he spoke in tongues more than all of them! His point was that when you are dealing with people, your concern is to edify them. Sometimes we listen to preachers who go off on a tangent just to show what they know. That isn't edifying anybody.

This matters so much at home, with your spouse and your children. Often they already know what they did was wrong. Then we come and pile on, and pile on, and pile on. Be careful there. Proverbs 10:11 says the mouth of the righteous is a well of life. Life is supposed to come forth from our mouths, and with that life you can dispel darkness and bring encouragement. Proverbs 12:18 says there is one who speaks like the piercings of a sword. Don't use your words to damage people. Use them to build people up.

So many people have spoken curses over their spouse and their children and made life harder because they were careless with their words. Especially those in authority: fathers, husbands, be very careful how you speak, because God respects the authority He gave you. Don't misuse it. Many people have let the devil into their home because of what they said.

5. Be Slow to Anger

James 1:19 also says to be slow to wrath. So often, hurtful words come out in anger. When you are angry, you don't have to say everything that comes to your mind. Chances are it isn't being inspired by the Holy Spirit! Calm down. Go cry it out, go pray it out, take a walk, and come back. The right words will come.

You don't want your flesh inspiring your words, because your words create. They can create life or death, light or darkness. Words are spiritual, and the realm of the spirit responds to them. Make sure the words you speak bring healing and bring life.

6. Be Clear

Be clear and concise with your words. And don't try to win every argument or prove you are always right. Some conversations, it is okay to just walk away from. If it's clear the other person only wants to be right, you don't need to keep going.

One thing we love about Jesus is how clear He was. Clear about His expectations of people. Clear about His promises. He said, "Let your yes be yes and your no be no." Today some ministers try to be very ambiguous about the things of God. Some people talk like politicians who never want to offend anybody, so everybody thinks they are on their side. Well, if all of us are in different camps, somebody is lying here!

For if the trumpet makes an uncertain sound, who will prepare for battle?

1 Corinthians 14:8 (NKJV)

Let your words be clear and easy to understand. You don't have to win the argument. Just make sure people know where you stand. You don't have to make people believe what you believe, but be clear on your position.

Bringing it back home: one thing spouses like to do is, for lack of a better term, force the other one to read their mind. There is no need for that! You can avoid so much conflict by simply saying, "This is what I need. These are my expectations." That alone can remove half the problem. It's Valentine's Day and you want something along certain lines? Please be clear. They cannot read your mind. It's your birthday and what you really want is a getaway, for you and your husband or just for you? Be clear, please! He or she cannot read your mind. Same with your children. Let's at least start there. When you aren't clear about what you think or what you want, you open the door to conflict and offense.

7. Speak the Truth in Love

The truth can be offensive, but we should not be. Look at the life of Jesus. Sometimes people were offended by what He said. It was true, but they weren't offended by how He said it. They were offended by what He said. Sometimes the truth just makes us uncomfortable.

So when someone says something to us and it comes out harsh, the first question to ask is, "Is it true?" Then we can decide whether it could have been said better.

The Bible says to speak the truth in love (Ephesians 4:15). Not to be loving and flatter people. And not just to speak the truth. Speak the truth in love. So many Christians are hurt because people just don't tell them the truth. There are so many false and unrealistic expectations because we overpromise what we can do or will do. One of the worst things you can do to someone is lie to them and then have them build their faith on that lie.

My son, do not despise the chastening of the Lord, Nor be discouraged when you are rebuked by Him; For whom the Lord loves He chastens.

Hebrews 12:5-6 (NKJV)

God speaks the truth to us in love, and we owe that to one another. That can sound like a strong statement, but love is not always "nice." In love, you sometimes rebuke people, because you need them to wake up. It's like an infection. Sometimes a doctor has to cut away part of the skin to remove it. That's painful, but if the infection isn't removed, the person could die.

It's amazing how some people are part of a Christian community where nobody tells them the truth, so they go through the same cycle over and over and over again. They don't have to! They could snap out of it. But nobody says anything because nobody wants anyone offended. If we can create an atmosphere where we tell each other the truth and nobody takes offense, it will resolve so much conflict.

Be ready to receive the truth too

Kemi: I tell people all the time, I would hate to leave my house with a big stain on my dress because my own child was afraid to say, "Mommy, your dress is stained." Then outsiders see you, they don't say anything, and they laugh behind your back. All because you never gave the people closest to you the freedom to tell you the truth.

So it is not only about speaking the truth in love. It's about being ready to receive the truth when it comes your way.

Here is one quick secret: ask for feedback. "What did you think of my presentation? Could I have said that better? What did you think of how I explained this?" Say it over and over to the people around you, and it gives them freedom to share. You will learn things you never even considered. Make yourself easy to approach.

We would rather hear the truth to our face, even if it isn't polished, than a lie with a smile. Tell us the truth, even if it comes out a little harsh. We'll go and cry, but we will receive it. Of course, the better place is truth spoken in love. But we still want the truth. We tell our children all the time, "Share! We want your feedback." If you don't live in our house, it might sound like, "Wow, they tell it as it is over there!" That's how we have trained them, and they hear it as it is from us too.

That freedom is the kind of relationship the Lord wants with us. He wants people to be free with Him, so that He can tell them the truth.

Practice These This Week

  • Listen for the why. In your next disagreement, ask what is behind your spouse's words.
  • Confirm before you respond: "Here is what I heard. Is that right?"
  • Pause before you speak, and if you are angry, take a walk before you answer.
  • Say what you need clearly instead of expecting your spouse to read your mind.
  • Ask someone close to you for honest feedback, and thank them for it.

Let's Pray

We want to pray James 1:19 over you, because we believe it is one of the best secrets to communication. Heavenly Father, we pray for everyone reading this. Give them ears to hear. Make them swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger. Help us all become more effective in receiving words and in giving words, and when we speak, let us speak to edify. We ask for Your help and Your grace to master this skill, in Jesus' name. Amen.

You now have several tools for communication. Don't just read them. Practice them!
