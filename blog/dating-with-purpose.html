Dating With Purpose: Clear Signals, Clear Expectations, and the Test of Grace

By Tayo and Kemi · October 13, 2026 · 13 min read

In this article

  • However It Starts, Run It by the Lord
  • Send a Clear Signal
  • Remember Why You Are Dating
  • Set Expectations Up Front
  • Dating Is a Testing Season
  • Questions to Ask This Week
  • Let's Pray

In our Roadmap from Single to Married series, we started at the beginning of the road. God has somebody for you. You need to understand who God created you to be, understand your purpose, and have your own relationship with the Father, because that helps with life in general and helps you see that certain people just will not work for you. And God is truly invested in you marrying the right person.

Now let's talk about the next stretch of the road. You think you may have found someone. What happens between "I think this could be it" and actually getting married?

However It Starts, Run It by the Lord

Sometimes you hear that voice, or you just have a knowing in your heart, or a dream. But sometimes it doesn't start in a way that looks "spiritual" at all. You take a class together and start being fond of each other. When you talk, there is just a flow. You keep bumping into each other! There are so many different stories like that. But no matter how you get connected, make sure you run it by the Lord and get His approval. That is very important.

Pay attention to the people you flow with. We run into people who have someone they flow with and never even consider that person. Some things God reveals to you first, and then you pursue. Other things you find out first: you just flow with this person, and then you go and pray about it.

We have said this before, and we'll say it again: it's easier to fall in love with somebody you flow with than to flow with somebody you're already in love with. Some people are attracted to someone, but the flow isn't there. If the flow is there, attraction can be developed by the grace of God. Now, there is nothing wrong with starting out attracted to someone. Either way, you still have to run it by the Lord, and He will lead you. The Lord is your Shepherd. He leads you beside still waters and in paths of peace. So if God is leading and there is no peace, God is not in it.

Send a Clear Signal

Let's use our proverbial John and Mary. Sometimes John prays and knows it's Mary, and Mary prays and knows it's John. That's a lot easier! But often one person has that knowing before the other. So how do you find out whether the other person is interested before you go into a relationship?

It's simple. You send a signal that you're interested. If the signal is received and reciprocated, you take action. If not, maybe they aren't interested, or maybe they aren't ready. Initially you might be subtle. But if you are interested, be clear. Let them know.

Now here is the flip side:

  • Don't send signals to several people at once. That's not right.
  • If you are not interested, don't raise someone's hopes. Understand boundaries. Don't give someone undue attention.

Tayo: Here is a practical example. A guy calls a lady every night, but he's not interested. It's been a month, two months, and she thinks he's interested in her, but he hasn't said anything. That's not right. To me, that's taking advantage of somebody. "But I didn't say anything!" No, you didn't say anything, but you are acting the way interested people act. You marked the territory. There may be other men interested in her, but because of the attention you are giving her, she isn't entertaining them. And you are not doing anything.

If you are on the receiving end, ask

So what we tell a lot of people is this: if someone has been giving you attention, being extra nice to you, which is good and appropriate, but it's been going on for over a month and they aren't being clear, ask. Don't let somebody pull you along and do nothing.

Over the years we have seen more women get hurt by this. We know people where a guy has been around for a year. You ask him, "Is anything going on?" "No, we're just friends." You ask her: "No, we're just friends." And sometimes she is lying to herself. She says "just friends," but in her heart she is hoping it becomes more. The way to handle that is not by saying "we're just friends." It's by asking the person to make a decision, and trusting God enough that if they say, "I'm not interested," fine. God can speak to them later when they see what they lost, or God can bring somebody better.

Don't give privileges that haven't been earned

We heard this and have come to realize it's true: many people get hurt in relationships because they give someone privileges that person didn't earn. Then when the relationship doesn't materialize, they feel used.

There are certain things you should only give your spouse, and physical intimacy is one of them. Sometimes people get into physical intimacy with someone they are dating, it doesn't end in marriage, and now they feel foolish and used. The same thing happens with commitment. We have had ladies who cook for the guy, clean, iron, do all these things, and he isn't making any commitment. "We're just friends." No, that's more than just friends. Talking every night is more than just friends!

Tayo: If you aren't bold enough to ask, I hope you have people in your community you can talk to. I tell the singles in our church, "If somebody has been marking your territory and you don't know what's going on, come talk to me. I'll go and talk to them. I will ask bluntly, because I don't want you to be hurt." I don't want somebody raising your hopes for six months, and then one day showing up with, "Oh, this is my girlfriend," and you are devastated. That happens a lot in church. I've been to churches where everybody begins to pick sides and it becomes so dysfunctional, the singles fighting one another, because things weren't handled appropriately.

Remember Why You Are Dating

Remember the title of this series: from single to married. You get into a relationship for the purpose of marriage. Yes, to develop a friendship. Yes, to get to know each other more. But with the intention of potentially getting married. And somewhere along the road you may realize you started the journey with the wrong person. There is nothing wrong with saying, "Oops, we have to walk back to the beginning."

We live in a generation where a lot of people don't hold biblical values, but as believers we need to. The purpose of a relationship is to head toward marriage. So if you know you cannot or will not marry this person, it's not right to be in a relationship with them, even if you both agree you're not getting married. There's nothing wrong with being single. It's okay to have friends. But there are boundaries: this is friendship, not romantic friendship.

Watch out for what we call the undefined relationship. People start hanging out, and all of a sudden they decide, "I guess we've been hanging out, we might as well be together," without ever evaluating it. Now, don't get us wrong. Things can start that way. You can be hanging out and find out you just flow, and you feel like talking. But if it begins to grow, pause and evaluate. Could this lead to marriage? Just because it started that way doesn't mean it has to continue that way. Set boundaries so neither of you gets hurt.

Don't skip steps

It's almost like there are graduations in this journey from dating to marriage. First, you commit to each other: "I'm your girlfriend, you're my boyfriend." Clear commitment, clear boundary. From there, hopefully you get a mentor, or a few mentors, to walk with you. And you don't skip steps. Some people say, "After all, we're dating, so we can be at each other's homes until one in the morning," or, "We can move in together, we're dating." No.

Set Expectations Up Front

Once you are in the relationship, talk about expectations up front. What are we each looking to get out of this? That's another filter. If it becomes clear one person just wants exclusive benefits, "so I can have somebody to sleep with," well, "I don't do that. I'm a believer, and I'm waiting until I get married." Have those conversations. "I'm looking to get to know you more, and I hope you get to know me more." Establish those things at the start so you both understand what you are shooting toward.

One thing we love about Jesus is that He always set expectations, so nobody was confused about what He expected of them. In Luke 14, great multitudes were following Him, in awe of Him. You could say they were sending Him signals! And He turned and told them plainly what it would cost to be His disciple.

For which of you, intending to build a tower, does not sit down first and count the cost, whether he has enough to finish it, lest, after he has laid the foundation, and is not able to finish, all who see it begin to mock him, saying, 'This man began to build and was not able to finish'?

Luke 14:28-30 (NKJV)

Setting expectations keeps you from starting something you can't finish. It's possible you start out thinking this is the right person and it turns out not to be. But at least you both started out believing it was right for both of you. We don't play with people's emotions.

How long should you date?

Tayo: It depends. But this is just Pastor Tayo's judgment: I don't think a relationship should go more than about three years unless there's a very good reason. My reason? Jesus spent only three years with His disciples! Sometimes there are circumstances. People live in different countries, or there are papers to file, and I understand that. But generally, three years is enough to understand each other, ask your questions, and know whether you will spend the rest of your lives together. Then the next phase is planning a wedding and being married.

A word to the ladies

Usually women get hurt more than men, and ladies, you don't have the luxury of jumping in and out of relationships the way some men do. If a guy doesn't know what he wants, fine. Let him go and find out what he wants, on his own time, not on yours. Remember, other people are available. You don't want somebody to take you off the market and then return you to the market after other potential husbands have passed by. Most respectable men, the kind who make good husbands, will not intrude when they see another man giving you attention and you responding.

And if you are not interested, send a clear signal. If they don't respect it, send it a little stronger. If they still don't respond, get people in your community to talk to them. We have had to talk to some guys: "Look, she is not interested. Stop hanging around her." Hopefully it doesn't come to that.

Don't rule out "not my type" too quickly

We say this to everybody. If someone loves God, is spiritually mature, has their natural life together, the two of you flow, and they express interest in you, you really should pray about it. They might not be your "type," in quotes. But don't rule them out until you have asked God. Some of us, our taste has been infected. You need to see things from God's point of view.

Dating Is a Testing Season

Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies. Test all things; hold fast what is good.

1 Thessalonians 5:19-21 (NKJV)

Don't despise what the Lord is telling you. But even when you believe it's the Lord, test all things. The whole dating and courtship period is a testing period. As one thing checks out, you hold fast. Another thing checks out, you hold fast some more. So it's not enough to know, "I'm supposed to be in a relationship with this person." Keep praying through the whole journey, especially in the early parts.

Kemi: I tell people, hopefully you're sure by the time you're buying your wedding dress! But even then, if things come up, you are still testing. I was once in a relationship where I started out thinking this was it. I had peace, and I thought it was of the Lord. About six months in, I realized, "Ooh, I actually missed God in this one." And it wasn't that he did anything wrong. I just kept praying, and the more I prayed, the more it was almost like the Lord was distant, and I knew it wasn't it. God said, "That's not your husband. I did not send you there." I repented, and I apologized to him. I cried! But I stood corrected. God was not in it.

The test of grace

One of the most important tests for a relationship is the test of grace. If God is in something, there is grace there. If God is not there, there is no grace. If you are in a relationship and you are forcing everything, with no evidence of God's grace, something is wrong. Either God didn't say you should do it, or you went ahead of His timing, or you are both in it but doing things God told you not to do. God will always give grace to what He is interested in.

So ask yourselves: since we've been in this relationship, is there evidence of God's divine favor and blessing? Is His light shining on it? One thing the light of God does is reveal things. As you go deeper, is His light showing you good things, or things that concern you?

Questions to Ask This Week

  • Have I run this connection by the Lord, and do I have peace about it?
  • Are my signals clear, or am I giving attention without commitment?
  • Have we defined the relationship and talked honestly about expectations and boundaries?
  • Is there evidence of God's grace on this relationship, or are we forcing it?

Let's Pray

Heavenly Father, we pray for Your people, for the grace of spiritual discernment: the ability to see the true nature of things. By Your light, let things be revealed, so they know, "This is it," or, "This is not it," and know how to walk. Keep them from jumping ahead of Your process; help them stay with You and proceed from there. Father, for those who have been hurt, or who took a wrong turn, we pray for grace to heal and grace to return to where they ought to be, so they walk in Your best in their relationships. We pray that everyone reading this experiences Your blessing in their relationship, in Jesus' name. Amen.
