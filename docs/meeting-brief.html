2

The honest framing

The temptation is to present this as pretty custom site versus functional Squarespace. That framing is wrong, and the preview page proves it: the approved design can live inside Squarespace.

So the real question is narrower and easier to answer:

Do you want to keep the tools Squarespace gives you, or take on replacing them in exchange for lower running costs and full control?

3

The two options

Option A

Stay on Squarespace

What they keep

  • Store and checkout for direct sales
  • Acuity scheduling, already connected
  • Form storage and notifications
  • Email Campaigns and the subscriber list
  • The member Login area
  • Editing their own content, no developer

What it costs them

  • The Squarespace subscription continues
  • Some design detail is harder to hold exactly
  • Conditional form fields are not supported natively
Option B

Move to the custom build

What they gain

  • Hosting cost drops to zero
  • The design exactly as approved
  • Noticeably faster pages
  • Accessibility verified by automated checks
  • No platform lock-in

What they give up

  • Store and checkout, needs replacing
  • Scheduling, needs replacing
  • Form handling, needs replacing
  • Email Campaigns, needs replacing
  • Editing without a developer

Recommendation

Stay on Squarespace for Phase 1

Not because the custom build is worse. Because leaving Squarespace means quietly signing up to rebuild four systems that currently work, none of which are in the $400 scope, six days before the intended launch.

The custom build already did its job. It settled the design, proved the content structure, and it stays available if they outgrow Squarespace later. Nothing about recommending A wastes it.

5

How to say it

Opening

We built the whole thing two ways so you could see it properly rather than imagine it. Let me show you the design first, then where it lives.

If they love the custom build and want to switch

It is genuinely the nicer site, and I am glad you like it. Before we commit though: your store, your booking system, your contact form and your mailing list all live in Squarespace today. Moving means rebuilding all four. That is real work and it is outside what we agreed, so I would rather we do it deliberately than discover it after the domain moves.

If they ask why not just use the free hosting

Hosting is the cheap part. The subscription is buying you the store, scheduling, forms and campaigns. And you are paid up to July next year, so the saving this year is smaller than it looks.

If they worry the Squarespace version will look worse

That is exactly why I built the preview page. Same design, running on your real site, inside your real header and footer. You are not choosing between good design and working tools.

Closing

My recommendation is we finish on Squarespace, get you launched, and keep the custom build on the shelf. If you outgrow the platform in a year, it is already built and waiting.

Raise these, do not wait to be asked

The Appointments page has prices nobody approved. Acuity is connected and currently shows Free Consultation, 30 minutes plus Basic Service $99 and Advanced Service $199. Those look like Acuity's sample entries. The agreed consultation is 20 minutes, and paid coaching is not in Phase 1. The page is disabled, so it is one toggle away from showing clients pricing that was never agreed. Needs correcting before launch.

Paid coaching is still an open scope decision. Six appointment types, one-coach versus two-coach rates, 50% deposits, balance collection, refund rules, Stripe and PayPal, per-session Zoom links. That is materially larger than the "20 minute consultation setup" in the $400 agreement. It needs its own quote, and it should not be promised in this meeting.

The 31 August target is at risk, and not because of us. The blockers below are all things only Zoe Life can supply. Worth agreeing a realistic date in the room.
